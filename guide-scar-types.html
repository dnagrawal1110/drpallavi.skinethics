HOW TO CHECK YOUR SCARS

Stand near a window or use your phone torch from the side. Side lighting shows the depth and edges of scars far better than light from the front. Take photos so you can compare later.

THE MAIN SCAR TYPES
📍
Type 01

Ice Pick Scars

What they look like

Narrow, deep pits, like the skin was pricked with a sharp tool. Small on the surface but deep underneath.

Commonly treated with

TCA CROSS, where a strong acid is applied carefully inside each scar to build collagen. Sometimes punch techniques.

⬜
Type 02

Boxcar Scars

What they look like

Round or oval dips with sharp, steep edges, a bit like chickenpox marks. Can be shallow or deep.

Commonly treated with

CO2 fractional laser, MNRF, and TCA CROSS for deeper ones.

🌊
Type 03

Rolling Scars

What they look like

Wide, soft dips with sloping edges that make the skin look wavy and uneven. They show most in side light.

Commonly treated with

Subcision, which releases the bands pulling the skin down, often combined with MNRF or CO2 laser.

⛰️
Type 04

Raised Scars

What they look like

Firm, raised bumps (hypertrophic scars or keloids), more common on the jawline, chest and back.

Commonly treated with

Steroid injections, silicone gels and some lasers. These are treated very differently from pitted scars.

NOT A SCAR? MARKS VS SCARS
Red or brown marksFlat marks left after a pimple heals. They usually fade with time, sunscreen and treatment
True scarsA change in skin texture, either a dip or a bump. They need procedures to remodel
TREATMENT MAP
CO2 fractional laserBoxcar and mixed scars. Has downtime
MNRFBoxcar, rolling and mixed scars. Lower pigmentation risk
SubcisionRolling scars that are tethered down
TCA CROSSIce pick and deep, narrow boxcar scars

Most people have a mix of types, so a combination plan usually works best.

"Scars don't fill in one session."

The realistic goal is significant improvement, not perfect skin. And active acne needs to be controlled first.
