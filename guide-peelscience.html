The Chemical Peel Guide

It isn't burning. It isn't damage. A chemical peel is the most controlled method of removing what's broken to reveal what's intact beneath it.

Think of It Like Stripping Old Paint from a Wall

Imagine a wall with five layers of old, cracked, discoloured paint. You could keep applying a fresh coat over the top — but the wall will never look smooth, because you're painting over the problem rather than fixing it.

🧱
Before
Damaged layers accumulated on top — new paint won't fix the texture
→
✨
After
Old layers removed — fresh, smooth surface exposed for real renewal

A chemical peel does exactly this for skin. By applying a precisely controlled acid solution, it dissolves the bonds holding damaged, dead, or discoloured cells to the surface — allowing them to shed in an organised, predictable manner and revealing the healthier skin beneath.

"The goal isn't to remove skin indiscriminately. It's to remove exactly the right layers — at exactly the right depth — to trigger the renewal you want."

Peel Depth — The Most Important Variable

All chemical peels are not the same. Depth determines what the peel can treat, the downtime involved, and the risks. Matching the right depth to the right concern is the clinical skill.

Type 01
Superficial

Epidermis Only

The most common peel. Removes the outermost dead cell layer. Zero to 2 days of mild flaking. Can be done monthly. Ideal for maintenance, glow, and early pigmentation.

Glycolic 20–35%, Lactic, Mandelic, Salicylic 20–30%
Type 02
Medium

Into the Upper Dermis

Addresses moderate pigmentation, acne marks, and fine lines. Expect 5–7 days of visible peeling and redness. Requires doctor supervision and pre-treatment protocol.

TCA 15–35%, Jessner's solution, Jessner's + TCA
Type 03
Deep

Mid-Dermis

Reserved for severe sun damage or deep wrinkling. 10–14 days downtime, significant redness. Rarely used on Indian skin due to high PIH risk. Requires full medical setting.

Phenol — used selectively, lighter skin types only

What Chemical Peels Can Treat

Sun Damage & Dark Spots
Acid exfoliation disperses melanin deposits and accelerates their clearance from the skin surface.
Acne & Clogged Pores
Salicylic acid is oil-soluble — it penetrates the pore lining to dissolve sebum and dead cell plugs from inside.
Uneven Texture & Dullness
Removing dead cell buildup immediately improves light reflection — the skin looks brighter after even a single session.
Fine Lines
Medium-depth peels stimulate new collagen formation in the dermis, softening fine surface lines over multiple sessions.
Melasma & Pigmentation
Mandelic and azelaic acid peels address melanin overproduction with lower PIH risk on Indian skin tones.
Post-Acne Marks (PIH)
Regular superficial peels accelerate PIH clearance significantly faster than topicals alone.

Recovery — Day by Day

Recovery from a superficial peel (the most common type) follows a predictable pattern:

Day 0 — Immediately After
Warmth & Mild Redness
The treated area feels warm, looks slightly flushed. Completely normal. A recovery serum and SPF are applied before leaving the clinic.
Days 1 – 2
Skin Looks Normal or Slightly Dry
Superficial peels have minimal visible downtime. Medium peels may show more pronounced redness. Moisturise frequently, no actives.
Days 3 – 5
Peeling Begins
Dry flakes appear as the treated layer sheds. This is expected — it is the old surface making way for new skin. Do not pick or force the peeling; let it shed naturally.
Days 6 – 7
Fresh Skin Revealed
Peeling is complete. The new skin beneath is fresh, smoother, and brighter. A slight pink tone fades within days. This is the result.

Common Myths

Chemical peels permanently thin the skin.
Peels remove only the damaged dead cell layer — the healthy living epidermis below is untouched. With medium and deep peels, the collagen stimulation that follows actually thickens the dermis over time.
Indian skin cannot have chemical peels.
Indian skin requires careful acid selection and pre-treatment preparation — but superficial peels with mandelic, lactic, and low-strength glycolic acid are routinely and safely performed. Expert selection is essential.
You can get the same result with a home peel.
OTC products contain acids at 2–5% concentration. Clinical peels use 20–35%+, with controlled application time, neutralisation, and post-care management. They are fundamentally different treatments.
After a peel, you're sensitive to sun forever.
Sensitivity is temporary during the healing window. Once healed, the new skin is no more sun-sensitive than before. Daily SPF is a good habit regardless — not a permanent restriction.
6

How to Prepare for Your First Peel

  • Stop retinoids and AHA/BHA products 5–7 days before the appointment
  • Avoid waxing or threading the treatment area within 1 week
  • Minimise sun exposure for 2 weeks before — tanned skin has higher PIH risk
  • Arrive with a clean, makeup-free face
  • Have SPF 50, a gentle cleanser, and a plain moisturiser ready at home
  • Avoid gym, steam, and swimming during the peeling phase (days 3–6)
  • Do not schedule a peel if you have active cold sores, open wounds, or very sensitive skin
