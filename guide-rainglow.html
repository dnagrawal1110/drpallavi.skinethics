Why Skin Loses Its Glow in Monsoon

You go indoors. Clouds cover the sun. You stop wearing sunscreen. And then the dullness, patches, and uneven tone show up within weeks — not from the rain, but from two triggers most people never suspect.

UV-A Doesn't Need Sunshine

This is the most dangerous monsoon skin myth: that cloudy weather means UV protection is unnecessary. The truth is that clouds block UV-B (the burning rays) more effectively than they block UV-A — the rays responsible for deep skin damage, pigmentation, and accelerated ageing.

The Key Fact
UV-A penetrates cloud cover at up to 80% of its full-sun intensity
UV-A has a longer wavelength than UV-B. It passes through clouds, glass, and even light clothing. It goes deeper into the dermis, directly stimulating melanin overproduction.
80%
UV-A through clouds

Every day you go out without SPF in monsoon — even when it looks overcast — you are accumulating UV-A exposure that darkens existing spots, triggers new pigmentation, and breaks down collagen in the dermis.

"No sunscreen in monsoon is arguably more damaging than no sunscreen in summer — because you forget about the UV that's still there."

The Two Triggers of Monsoon Pigmentation

☀️
Trigger 01
UV-A Through Clouds
Continuously stimulates melanocytes to produce melanin. The lack of burning sensation removes the warning signal — UV-A damage accumulates silently.
🍄
Trigger 02
Malassezia Yeast
A naturally present skin yeast that overgrows in warm, humid conditions. Malassezia releases compounds that disrupt melanin regulation, causing hyperpigmentation patches — especially on the forehead, cheeks, and chest.

Most pigmentation advice addresses only UV. In monsoon, the Malassezia component is equally important — and requires a completely different treatment approach. Treating only one of two triggers produces partial results at best.

The Treatment That Addresses Both

Clinical Combination Protocol
Azelaic Acid Peels + Laser Toning
Azelaic Acid Peels
Azelaic acid has a dual mechanism: it inhibits tyrosinase (the enzyme that produces melanin) AND has documented antifungal action against Malassezia. This makes it uniquely suited to monsoon pigmentation — it addresses both triggers in a single ingredient. Also has anti-inflammatory and pore-clearing properties, making it ideal for acne-prone Indian skin.
Laser Toning (Q-Switch Nd:YAG)
Targets existing melanin deposits in the dermis with selective photothermolysis. Breaks melanin granules into fragments the lymphatic system can clear. Stimulates collagen production in the papillary dermis. When combined with Azelaic Acid peels (peel first, then laser after healing), the combination clears existing pigmentation while preventing new formation.

The sequencing matters: peels prime the skin and address new pigment formation; laser toning clears the deposits already accumulated. Results from a 3–4 session protocol are significantly better than either treatment alone.

The Monsoon Sunscreen Guide

SPF Number
Minimum SPF 50 — this is not summer-specific advice. SPF 50 filters 98% of UV-B. For UV-A, check for PA+++ rating.
Broad Spectrum
Must say "PA+++" or "broad spectrum" on the label. SPF alone only measures UV-B protection — not UV-A.
Formulation
Gel or fluid sunscreens for oily/combination skin in monsoon humidity. Cream formulations can occlude pores in high humidity.
Quantity
2 finger-lengths for the face. Most people apply 20–25% of the required amount — the SPF you receive depends on applying the amount tested.
Reapplication
Every 2–3 hours of outdoor exposure. Rain washes off physical sunscreens — reapply after getting wet.

Monsoon Glow Myths

Cloudy days mean no sunscreen needed.
Up to 80% of UV-A passes through cloud cover. UV-A is the primary cause of pigmentation and premature ageing — and it doesn't cause burning, so the damage accumulates invisibly.
Dullness in monsoon is just from humidity.
Dullness is primarily driven by accumulated dead cell buildup on the surface (exfoliation slows in humidity) combined with Malassezia overgrowth and UV-A triggered melanin deposits — humidity is the least of the three causes.
Vitamin C serums alone can fix monsoon pigmentation.
Vitamin C inhibits tyrosinase and fades mild surface pigmentation. It cannot address Malassezia-related pigmentation, nor can it clear deeper melanin deposits. It is a maintenance ingredient, not a treatment for active pigmentation.
Peels are not safe to do in monsoon because of humidity.
Superficial peels with the right acids (Mandelic, Azelaic) are well-tolerated in monsoon and highly effective for addressing exactly the triggers that worsen this season. The protocol is adapted — not stopped — for the season.
