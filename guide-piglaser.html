How Does Pigmentation Laser Work?

The laser doesn't burn your skin randomly. It finds dark targets with extraordinary precision — and leaves everything else completely untouched.

The Black Balloon Principle

Imagine a room full of balloons — black ones and white ones mixed together. If you shine a heat lamp into that room, the black balloons absorb the heat and pop. The white ones reflect the energy and remain completely intact.

Laser energy targets dark pigment — surrounding tissue absorbs nothing

Pigmentation laser works on exactly this principle. Melanin — the pigment responsible for dark spots — absorbs specific wavelengths of laser energy that normal surrounding skin reflects. The result is selective destruction of pigmented cells, with no collateral damage to healthy tissue around them.

This selectivity is what makes modern pigmentation lasers so precise — and so different from the burning, non-selective lasers of a generation ago.

"The key word is selective. A properly calibrated pigmentation laser does not harm the road to fix the pothole. It finds the pothole, and only the pothole."

2

The Science — Selective Photothermolysis

The formal term for the black balloon principle is Selective Photothermolysis — coined by dermatology researchers in 1983 and still the governing physics of modern laser treatment.

01

Wavelength Selection

Different laser wavelengths are absorbed by different chromophores (targets). The Q-Switched Nd:YAG at 1064nm and 532nm wavelengths are preferentially absorbed by melanin — the pigment in dark spots and tattoo ink.

02

Pulse Duration Matters

The laser fires in extremely short pulses — nanoseconds or picoseconds. The pulse must be shorter than the thermal relaxation time of the target (the time it takes to cool down) to destroy the target without heating surrounding tissue.

03

Melanin Absorbs, Fragments

The melanin granule absorbs the energy, heats instantly, and shatters into microscopic fragments. This photoacoustic effect physically breaks up the pigment deposit without burning the skin.

04

Immune System Clears the Debris

The fragmented melanin particles are small enough to be engulfed by macrophages — the body's waste disposal cells. They carry the pigment debris away through the lymphatic system over the following weeks. The spot fades gradually as clearance progresses.

3

What to Expect — Session by Session

Session 1

Initial Response

Mild redness and warmth immediately after. Some spots temporarily darken (this is normal — it's the shattered melanin before the immune system clears it). Skin returns to baseline within 24–48 hours.

Sessions 2–3

Visible Fading Begins

Most patients notice meaningful lightening of treated spots. Skin texture often improves alongside pigmentation reduction as the laser also stimulates collagen remodelling.

Sessions 4–6

Progressive Clearance

Deeper or more stubborn pigmentation responds over multiple sessions. Melasma, which is hormonally driven, typically requires more sessions and ongoing maintenance than sun-induced spots.

Maintenance

Long-Term Management

For melasma, sun protection and periodic maintenance sessions prevent recurrence. For discrete sun spots, clearance is often permanent — provided sun protection is maintained.

Which Pigmentation Types Respond Best?

Solar Lentigines (Sun Spots)
Discrete dark spots caused by cumulative UV exposure. The most predictable responder to laser.
Excellent
Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH)

Myths About Pigmentation Laser

Laser thins and permanently damages the skin.
Q-Switched lasers work at the cellular level — they do not ablate or thin the skin's surface. Properly calibrated sessions actually stimulate collagen, improving overall skin quality.
One session removes all pigmentation.
Melanin clearance is gradual — it depends on the depth of pigmentation, the number of sessions, and consistent sun protection between treatments. Most concerns require 4–8 sessions.
Laser is not safe for Indian skin.
The Q-Switched Nd:YAG 1064nm is considered the gold standard for darker Fitzpatrick skin types precisely because it has lower risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation than shorter wavelengths. Calibration and expertise matter.
After laser, you can't go in the sun ever again.
Sun protection is essential after laser — as it should be for everyone year-round. SPF 50 applied correctly is all that's required. You don't need to avoid daylight permanently.
6

Before & After Your Session

  • Avoid direct sun exposure for 2 weeks before treatment — tan skin has higher PIH risk
  • Discontinue retinoids and AHAs 5–7 days before the session
  • Apply SPF 50 every morning for at least 4 weeks pre and post treatment
  • Some spots may darken in the first 5–7 days post laser — this is normal clearance
  • Avoid gym, steam, and swimming for 48–72 hours after each session
  • Do not pick or scratch the treated area — let the pigment fragment and clear naturally
