The Hair Transplant Guide

Moving hair from where it survives to where it's needed — and why the donor site is the most important decision in the entire procedure.

Think of It Like Transplanting Plants in a Garden

When a section of your garden soil is infertile — nothing you plant there grows — the solution isn't to keep trying different seeds in the same bad soil. You take healthy plants from a fertile patch and relocate them to where growth is needed.

🌱
Donor Zone
DHT-resistant follicles from the back and sides of the scalp
→
🌳
Recipient Zone
Thinning or bald areas where DHT has destroyed the original follicles

Hair transplantation works on the same logic. Follicles from the back and sides of the scalp are genetically programmed to be resistant to DHT — the hormone that causes hair loss. When transplanted to a balding area, they retain this resistance and continue growing for life.

"A transplant doesn't create new hair. It relocates hair that is guaranteed to survive — from a zone where it can be spared to a zone where it's needed."

2

Why Some Hair Is DHT-Resistant

Androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss) is driven by DHT — Dihydrotestosterone, a derivative of testosterone. DHT binds to receptors in genetically susceptible follicles, causes them to miniaturise, and eventually stops them from producing hair altogether.

The follicles at the back and sides of the scalp (the "safe donor zone") carry different androgen receptor genetics — they do not respond to DHT in the same way. This resistance is permanent and is preserved even after transplantation. The relocated follicles grow exactly as they did in the donor site.

FUE vs FUT — Understanding the Techniques

Technique A
FUE
MethodIndividual follicles extracted one by one with a micro-punch tool (0.8–1mm diameter)
ScarringTiny dot scars, invisible at normal hair length
Recovery5–7 days. Can return to work quickly
Best forMost patients; preferred when short hair is worn
Technique B
FUT
MethodA strip of scalp is removed from the donor zone; follicles are dissected from it under microscope
ScarringLinear scar at back of head, hidden by hair
Recovery10–14 days. Suture removal required
Best forLarge sessions needing maximum graft yield

The choice between FUE and FUT depends on the extent of hair loss, donor density, the patient's lifestyle, and how they wear their hair. Neither technique is universally superior — the right choice is case-specific.

Are You a Good Candidate?

✅
Good candidate: Stable hair loss pattern (not actively progressing rapidly), adequate donor density at the back/sides, realistic expectations about coverage, age typically 25 and above
✅
Good candidate: Hair loss due to pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia), not scarring alopecia or active autoimmune conditions
✅
Good candidate: Non-smoker or willing to stop 2 weeks before and after surgery (smoking impairs follicle survival)
❌
Not ideal: Very young patients (under 25) with rapidly progressing loss — the final pattern isn't established yet, making hairline design unreliable
❌
Not ideal: Insufficient donor density — if the donor zone is too thin, there aren't enough follicles to achieve meaningful coverage without depleting it
❌
Not ideal: Active alopecia areata, trichotillomania, or scarring alopecias — transplants into inflamed or autoimmune-active scalp have poor survival

Month-by-Month Recovery

Days 1 – 10

Initial Healing

Tiny scabs form at each graft site. Mild swelling around the forehead is common and resolves in 3–5 days. Grafts are delicate — no touching, rubbing, or direct water pressure on the transplanted area.

Weeks 2 – 6

Shock Loss — Expected and Temporary

The transplanted hairs shed. This alarms almost every patient — but it is completely normal. The follicle itself remains alive beneath the scalp. The shed hair will regrow from the same follicle.

Months 3 – 4

New hair begins emerging from the transplanted follicles. Initially fine and unpigmented — it thickens and darkens progressively. Patience is critical here; impatience is the most common patient complaint at this stage.

Months 6 – 8

Visible Coverage

Significant growth now apparent. Most patients are satisfied at this point. Hair continues to thicken and the texture normalises to match surrounding hair.

Month 12 – 18

Final Result

Full maturation of all transplanted grafts. The final density, texture, and hairline are now visible. Any assessment of the result should be made at this point — not earlier.

6

Why Hairline Design Is as Important as the Surgery

A technically perfect transplant with a poorly designed hairline produces an unnatural result that is obvious and permanent. The hairline must account for:

  • The patient's age — a hairline that looks natural at 30 may look unnatural at 55
  • Facial proportions — the hairline must fit the forehead shape and facial geometry
  • Projected future loss — space must be left for follicles that may still be lost
  • Irregular micro-variation — natural hairlines are never perfectly straight, transplanted ones shouldn't be either
7

Common Myths

Transplanted hair falls out after a few years.
Transplanted follicles retain their DHT resistance permanently. They grow for life in the new location — the same way they grew in the donor site for decades.
You can transplant unlimited hair to cover any bald area.
The donor zone is finite. Donor management — using only what can be spared without depleting the safe zone — is one of the most critical technical skills in hair transplantation.
The result looks obvious and fake.
Modern FUE with skilled hairline design is undetectable even on close inspection. The "plug" look was a characteristic of outdated multi-graft techniques from the 1980s–90s.
