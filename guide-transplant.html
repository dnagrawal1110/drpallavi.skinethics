THINK OF IT LIKE A GARDEN
1

The back of the garden is healthy

The back and sides of your scalp hold hair that usually resists pattern hair loss. This is the donor area.

2

The front has some empty patches

The hairline, front and crown are where thinning shows. This is the recipient area.

3

Healthy plants are moved, not created

Follicles are carefully taken from the donor area and placed where hair has thinned. They keep the strength of the area they came from.

THE TWO THINGS THAT DECIDE YOUR RESULT
🌳
Most Important

The Quality of Your Donor Area

Why It Matters

Every transplanted hair comes from your donor area, and that supply is limited. Its density, the thickness of each hair and how stable it is decide how much coverage is possible.

What Your Doctor Checks

How many hairs grow per area at the back and sides, how thick they are, and whether that area is also thinning.

✏️
Second

Hairline Design

Why It Matters

A well-designed hairline uses fewer grafts to give a natural, fuller look. It follows your face shape and age, with soft, slightly irregular edges rather than a straight line.

Good To Know

A hairline that looks right at 25 should still look right at 45. Good design plans for the future, not just for today.

ARE YOU A GOOD CANDIDATE?
✔ Usually a good fit
✔Pattern hair loss that has become fairly stable
✔A healthy, dense donor area
✔Good general health
✔Realistic expectations about density
✔Willing to continue medical treatment for existing hair
✕ Usually not right now
✕Active, sudden shedding (telogen effluvium)
✕Patchy hair loss (alopecia areata)
✕A thin or weak donor area
✕Hair loss from an untreated cause like thyroid or anaemia
✕Very early or fast-moving hair loss

HOW GRAFTS ARE PLANNED

A graft is a natural group of 1 to 4 hairs that grow together from the scalp. Grafts are moved as these groups so the result looks natural.

The number of grafts you need is decided in consultation, based on:

Area to coverHairline only, front, crown, or a combination
Donor supplyHow many grafts can be taken safely without thinning the back
Hair typeThick, wavy or curly hair gives more visual coverage per graft
Future hair lossDonor hair is kept in reserve in case thinning continues later
Priority areasThe hairline and front usually come first because they frame the face
RECOVERY & RESULTS — MONTH BY MONTH

Everyone heals at their own pace. This is a typical pattern, and your doctor will guide you at each stage.

Days 1–3

Rest and gentle care

Some swelling, redness and tenderness are common. Follow your doctor's instructions for sleeping position, medicines and the first wash.

Days 7–14

Small scabs settle

Tiny crusts around the grafts fall off with gentle washing. Most people return to desk work within days.

Weeks 2–8

The transplanted hairs shed

This is normal and expected. The hair shaft falls, but the root stays safely under the skin and rests before growing again.

Months 2–3

The quiet phase

The area may look like it did before surgery. This is the hardest part to wait through, and it's part of the process.

Months 3–5

New hairs start growing

Fine, thin hairs begin to appear. They are often soft at first and thicken over time.

Months 6–9

Visible change

Hairs get longer and thicker, and most people see a clear difference by now.

Months 12–18

Final result

The result is usually judged at about a year. The crown can take longer to fill in fully.

"Shedding after a transplant doesn't mean it failed. The roots are resting, not lost."

Transplanted hair follows a natural growth cycle. Patience through the first few months is part of getting a good result.

COMMON MYTHS
✕

"A transplant creates new hair"

It moves existing permanent follicles from one area to another. The total number of hairs on your head stays the same.

✕

"Anyone can get one"

It depends on the cause of your hair loss and the strength of your donor area. A proper diagnosis comes first.

✕

"Results show in a few weeks"

Transplanted hairs usually shed first. Real growth starts after a few months and the final result takes about a year.

✕

"After a transplant, I don't need any treatment"

Transplanted hair is long-lasting, but your other hair can keep thinning. Medical treatment protects it.

✕

"More grafts always means a better result"

Good planning and hairline design matter as much as numbers, and over-harvesting can thin the donor area.

AFTERCARE — THE FIRST FEW WEEKS

General guidance only. Always follow the exact instructions your doctor gives you.

✔ Do
✔Wash your scalp exactly as shown by the clinic
✔Sleep with your head slightly raised for the first few nights
✔Take your medicines as prescribed
✔Protect your scalp from direct sun
✔Keep your follow-up appointments
✕ Don't
✕Scratch or pick at the scabs
✕Wear tight caps or helmets until your doctor allows
✕Do heavy workouts or swim in the early days
✕Smoke, as it slows healing
✕Panic when the transplanted hairs shed
